# IkeOru — Moonshot Demo Script

> Learn. Do. Prove.

## Before the demo

- Use synthetic lead, candidate, and job-requirement data only.
- Confirm the visible run label is **LIVE**, **MOCK**, or **REPLAY** before describing a result.
- Use the live route only after a successful, separately recorded provider check. A configured key alone is not proof of live access.
- If showing prepared evidence, name it as prepared before opening it.

> **Current build note:** The local MVP provides ordered objective-led Academy sections, worked examples, common-mistake explanations, server-checked knowledge checks, constrained Labs, and one server-graded independent scenario per active path. Describe the result as introductory evidence; do not present it as broad competence certification.

## Primary route — about four minutes

### 0:00 — Problem and promise

“People may know automation matters, but often cannot break a work task into safe steps—or prove they can apply those steps. IkeOru closes that gap: Learn. Do. Prove.”

Show the dashboard. Point out seven domains: **Sales** and **HR** are active; Marketing, Finance, Operations, Customer Support, and Business Intelligence are visibly Coming Soon. Explain that the MVP deliberately focuses on two scoped learning loops rather than pretending to automate every business function.

If the first-visit tour appears, use it briefly: explain Learner versus Manager, the two active paths, and **Learn → Do → Prove**. Use **Next** to show the sequence, then **Finish tour**. The persistent **Help & tour** action can replay it without changing session or progress state.

### 0:25 — Sales: learn and practise

Open **Sales → Qualify a New Lead**. Start with the Academy lesson rather than the Run screen. Move through the objective-led sections, worked example, common-mistake guidance, and knowledge check. Explain the trigger, supplied input, AI interpretation, deterministic policy, permitted action, and human review boundary. A completed lesson is progress through the Academy, not yet a competence claim.

Move into the constrained Lab and explain how each choice maps back to the lesson. Complete the **Independent scenario** first: read the synthetic evidence, choose WARM rather than inventing urgency, and explain why missing timing and budget stay unknown. The server returns field-level reasoning feedback. Then review the supported run: a new lead is the trigger, synthetic lead details are input, AI interprets supplied business context, a deterministic server policy selects the permitted internal task, and incomplete information can go to human review.

Choose `New lead submitted`, choose a HOT follow-up rule, and load/enter a labelled synthetic lead. State that no real CRM, contact, or email is used.

### 1:05 — Sales: run and result

Run the workflow. Show the persisted timeline: validation, AI classification, policy evaluation, internal task/intentional skip, and saved result. Explain the supplied-input evidence, missing information, and selected branch. For a HOT result, show the internal task; for COLD, explain why a successful workflow intentionally creates none; for `NEEDS_REVIEW`, explain the review task.

Do not call a MOCK or REPLAY result live.

### 1:40 — Sales: assess and prove

Open the Sales assessment. Explain that the server grades both conceptual understanding and the practical rule exercise against the lesson objectives, then checks lesson completion and eligible run evidence before issuing a single achievement. A pass demonstrates this defined introductory capability; it is not a broad workplace certification. Show the private portfolio and explain its public-safe selection control. If showing a prepared award, clearly say it was prepared, not newly awarded by the displayed run.

### 2:15 — HR: learn and practise

Open **HR → Candidate Screening** and begin with its Academy lesson. Move through the objective-led sections, worked example, common-mistake guidance, and knowledge check. Explain the decision boundary before opening the Lab: it is a synthetic learning exercise, not an applicant-tracking system or a hiring decision tool. The learner compares supplied candidate information with explicit job-related requirements. The workflow shows evidence and missing information, and a human reviewer—not the system—owns the next employment decision.

Point out that protected characteristics and proxies are out of bounds. Complete the **Independent scenario** by identifying partial evidence and selecting mandatory human review; the server explains why a missing criterion is not an automatic rejection. Then load/enter a labelled synthetic candidate and job requirement example and make the constrained practice choices.

### 2:55 — HR: run, review, and prove

Run the HR workflow. Show the ordered timeline and structured result. Highlight evidence tied to supplied text, missing information left as unknown, and the mandatory human-review recommendation. Do not describe the result as an advance, rejection, ranking, or final hiring decision.

Open the distinct HR assessment and explain that it checks both the concepts and the practical human-review boundary. Achievement prerequisites are backend-enforced per path. Return to the portfolio to show that projects/achievements remain private unless the learner explicitly selects safe fields for sharing.

### 3:40 — Close

“IkeOru does more than teach automation vocabulary. It lets learners practise a bounded workplace workflow, inspect an honest result, and carry evidence of introductory capability forward—while keeping humans in control where judgment matters.”

## Provider-unavailable fallback

1. Say plainly: “The live provider is unavailable or not enabled, so I am using IkeOru’s labelled local fallback. It demonstrates workflow behaviour, not a successful live AI request.”
2. Show the visible **MOCK** or **REPLAY** label and run/load the deterministic synthetic Sales or HR example. Walk through validation, structured result, policy/review handling, and persisted timeline.
3. State the limitation: mock and replay do not satisfy live-run achievement prerequisites and cannot award a new achievement during this fallback.
4. If evidence screens are needed, open a separately labelled prepared learner profile. State that it is prepared evidence, not an achievement earned by the fallback run.
5. Continue with the privacy and human-review explanations; never silently substitute mock/replay for live.

## Recovery cues

- **Invalid input:** show the field error and say no adapter call is made.
- **Sales `NEEDS_REVIEW`:** show the internal review task and explain that the system does not invent certainty.
- **HR missing information:** show the gap and the human-review recommendation; do not imply an employment decision.
- **Provider/configuration error:** show the safe failure state, preserve entered data where available, and use the labelled fallback only if appropriate.
- **Session/error state:** explain the state honestly and never claim a completion, task, review recommendation, achievement, or portfolio update that was not persisted.
